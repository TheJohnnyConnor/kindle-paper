Canada · 25 years on · A2

Canadians Mark Remembrance Day 25 Years After Afghanistan Deployment Began

Canadians gather at cenotaphs across the country for Remembrance Day, marking 25 years since Canadian forces began operations in Afghanistan — roughly 40,000 members of the Canadian Armed Forces served and 159 were killed. The national ceremony at the National War Memorial in Ottawa included three Second World War veterans, with Prime Minister Mark Carney and Governor General Louise Arbour laying wreaths. The ceremonies come weeks after a fire at Canadian Forces Base Wainwright during a training exercise killed four soldiers and injured 39 others. Carney leaves today on a multi-country trip to Ireland and Asia aimed at trade and security talks.

Canada · Wartime service · A4

Veterans press Ottawa to recognize Afghanistan service as wartime

Veterans are pressing Ottawa to formally recognize Canada’s Afghanistan mission as wartime service as the federal government prepares public consultations on Bill S-246. The Senate bill, now before the House of Commons, would designate service in Afghanistan and the first Gulf War as wartime service; the deployment is currently classified as service in a “special duty area.” The debate comes in a year of anniversaries: 25 years since Canadian troops first arrived in Afghanistan, 20 years since Operation Medusa, 15 years since the end of Canada’s combat mission.
