Canada · Economy · A1

Bank of Canada raises key interest rate, says another hike possible

The Bank of Canada on Wednesday, October 28, hiked its key policy rate for the first time in more than three years, lifting it by 25 basis points to 2.5%, and said further increases might be needed to prevent soaring energy costs from fuelling broader inflation. The bank also raised its inflation forecast for 2027 to 3.1% from the 2.0% predicted in July, with growth this year set at 1.0%. Governor Tiff Macklem said an increase was needed to reduce the risk that elevated energy prices could spill over and become generalized inflation, and that there could be a need to increase the policy rate further, but the bank would proceed carefully given uncertainties. The annual inflation rate hit 3.2% in September, above the bank’s 1%-3% target range, almost solely led by higher energy and energy-related transportation costs; inflation was expected to end the year at 3.5% and stay around 3% for most of next year before returning close to the 2% target by mid-2028. The increase would likely push up bank lending rates and mortgage costs. It was the first rate hike since July 2023.
