Trades pain points

Late payment — carry a $30,000 job for six weeks out of your own pocket — “Ray the remodeler used to bill a $30,000 job all at the end. Six weeks of payroll, materials, and subs, all out of his pocket while he waited to get paid.” — @roguesconsulting. Why it matters: Billing at the end means the contractor is the bank for the whole job — six weeks of cash out before a dollar comes back. Staged billing (30% up front, 40% midpoint, 30% on completion) keeps the cash ahead of the work on the same job at the same price.

@roguesconsulting on Threads

Change orders — hating paperwork costs more than the paperwork does — “Construction contractors who hate paperwork usually hate unpaid extras even more.” — @montesinolaw. Why it matters: Skipping the change-order form feels like saving time on site; it is actually how the extra work ends up unpaid. The paperwork is the cheaper of the two hates.

@montesinolaw on Threads

Late payment — retainage with nobody chasing it — “Construction retainage should have a calendar, an owner, and a follow-up process.” — @montesinolaw. Why it matters: Retainage is money already earned, held back — and without a named owner and a date to chase it, it quietly becomes invisible after substantial completion. A held-back 10% with no follow-up process is a donation.

@montesinolaw on Threads

Scope creep — “can you just add this small thing?” — “Client: ‘Can you just add this small thing?’ That small thing is how a project quietly grows unpaid hours.” — @theprofreelancer. Why it matters: The small ask never arrives alone and never stays small. The fix is a boundary question before saying yes — does it change the outcome, the timeline, or the price? If yes, it gets written down as a change order.

Despite Hegseth memo, Canada hasn’t seen changes to defense relationship with US, says DefMin

Defence Minister David McGuinty said at the Halifax International Security Forum that Canada has not seen the classified memo U.S. Secretary of War Pete Hegseth reportedly sent imposing new restrictions on the U.S.-Canada defence relationship, first reported by The Washington Post on Friday. “It’s clear that there’s some sort of document we haven’t seen,” McGuinty said, but “Canadian-American cooperation on defense, security, and intelligence continues” and he has seen “no evidence of a change.” The Pentagon said the guidance does not affect defence intelligence cooperation, NORAD, or NATO, and U.S. Sen. Langley schools add 511 students this year, reaching 27,046

The Langley School District has 27,046 Ministry-funded students this school year, a headcount 511 higher than last year, Secretary-Treasurer Brian Iseli told the Board of Education at its regular meeting on Tuesday, Nov. 17. Regular enrolment came in 157 FTE above budget, worth a net $1,416,120 in funding, while English Language Learners were 186 below projection. Langley is the fifth-largest school district in B.C., with a 2025-26 annual budget of $436.3 million.
