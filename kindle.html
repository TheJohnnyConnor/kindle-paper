Canada · Budget passes · A3

Budget 2026 passes the Commons 162–151: what changes for you

MPs in the House of Commons voted 162 to 151 on Thursday evening, November 19, to pass the Liberal government’s 2026 federal budget, removing the threat of an early election over the spending plan. The budget, titled Maîtres Chez Nous (Masters of Our Destiny), was tabled November 5 by Finance Minister François-Philippe Champagne and sets out billions of dollars in new borrowing and tax measures aimed largely at stimulating infrastructure construction and other capital projects. It projects a $62.3-billion deficit this year and adds about $142 billion in new measures over five years. The vote does not put every measure into law yet — separate implementation legislation still has to pass Parliament. Reported knock-ons include another 50 per cent top-up to the GST rebate due early in 2027 and a new Build Canada Workers Benefit meant to help about 240,000 people out of poverty.

BC · New premier today · A4

B.C. Conservatives, including premier-designate Doerkson, take office oath

British Columbia’s incoming Conservative legislators took the oath of office at the legislature in Victoria on Thursday, in two ceremonies, completing the first step in a transfer of power that makes Lorne Doerkson the province’s 38th premier today, Friday, November 20, when he is sworn in along with his Conservative cabinet at Government House in Victoria. Doerkson told the newly sworn MLAs that voters want no excuses and the new government must deliver on its promises, and said it will pursue an ambitious, unprecedented economic agenda. The Conservatives won 64 of 93 seats in the October 24 snap election — B.C.’s first Conservative majority government in nearly 100 years and the province’s largest majority caucus in 25 years — though two judicial recounts are still pending (Kelowna-Centre and Ladysmith-Oceanside, counting from Nov 23). Doerkson, a former rodeo rider and newspaper publisher who became the party’s interim leader just days before the election was called, has signalled a return to office for public servants and daily singing of O Canada in schools, with more detail expected after the cabinet is sworn in. The new government will not recall the legislature for a fall sitting; a substantive legislative agenda is planned for the spring. Elected NDP and Green legislators take their oaths next week.

Langley · Council deferral · A7

New Langley Township council to take a closer look at transportation strategy

Langley Township council unanimously deferred endorsement of its updated Transportation and Mobility Strategy, a high-level plan covering roads, public transit, pedestrian and cycling lanes, and other mobility issues in the community. Staff will revise the report and bring it back with recommendations and an itemized list of targets for the next five years. The plan was presented to the previous council before the election but was deferred; it returned to the agenda at the council meeting on Monday, Nov 16, where newly elected Mayor Jay Lundgren called for another delay, saying the report is large and the new council should review it and have its questions answered together.
