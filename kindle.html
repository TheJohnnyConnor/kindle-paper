Canada (B.C.) · A3

King tides coincide with storms across coastal B.C. as evacuation alerts issued

King tides are coinciding with heavy rain and wind across coastal B.C., prompting coastal flooding warnings and emergency preparations. Environment and Climate Change Canada issued coastal flooding warnings for the west coast of Vancouver Island, Haida Gwaii and parts of the Central Coast; storm surges were expected to lift water levels 0.2 to 0.5 metres above usual on Wednesday and Thursday. Evacuation alerts went out for low-lying areas in the Tla-o-qui-aht First Nation and Parksville. In Metro Vancouver, Delta prepared low-lying coastal neighbourhoods for flood mitigation. Squamish declared a state of local emergency, with crews sandbagging and reinforcing dikes as high tides combined with already high water levels. Officials warned people to stay clear of rivers and not drive across flooded roads. The king-tide window was expected to end Thursday, Nov. 26.

Langley shelter opens 20 mats amid extreme weather alert

The Homelessness Services Association of BC issued an extreme weather alert for Langley, warning of several days of rain that could cause pooling and flooding; the alert, issued Nov. 10, remains in effect until cancelled. Gateway of Hope, at 5787 Langley Bypass, has opened 20 mats for people aged 19 and older experiencing homelessness, with dinner, breakfast and lunch, showers and laundry, until the site is full. The shelter is wheelchair accessible and accepts pets on a leash or in a kennel. People are advised to call 604-514-7375 for details; after 9 p.m., call the RCMP at 604-532-3200 for shelter help.
